# Code of conduct

This is a house document rather than an adopted one. It is short because a long
one on a project this size is a document nobody reads and nobody applies.

## What is expected

Argue about the work. A review says what is wrong with a change, what the
evidence is, and what would make it right, and it says the same thing whoever
wrote the change.

Say what you measured and what you assumed, and keep them apart. A claim
presented as a measurement is the defect this project cares most about, and
somebody who corrects one is doing the job.

Take a correction as a correction. Being wrong in public about a number is
ordinary here; the version of it that is a problem is arguing past evidence
rather than reading it.

Assume the other person read the issue and disagrees, before assuming they did
not read it.

## What is not

Harassment, in the ordinary meaning: sustained personal attack, sexualised
attention, slurs about who somebody is, publishing somebody's private details,
or following a person from thread to thread.

Making a technical argument about a person instead of about their work. That
includes the polite forms of it, which are the common ones.

Deliberately wasting other people's review time: a change nobody asked for
opened repeatedly, an argument reopened without new evidence, a body edited to
say something other than what was reviewed.

## Where it applies

The issue tracker, pull requests, commit messages and anything else tracked in
this repository. Nowhere else. What people do in other places is not this
document's business.

## Reporting

To the maintainer, privately, through https://github.com/iderex.

No other private channel is published, and that is a gap rather than a
statement that one is not needed. A report about the maintainer has nowhere to
go here, and this document does not pretend otherwise.

## What happens

The maintainer reads it and decides. The possible outcomes are a word in
private, a change reverted, a thread locked, and a block from the repository.
There is no committee, no appeal process and no fixed timeline, because
inventing three of those for a project with one maintainer would describe
something that does not exist.

Anything decided under this document that changes what is in the tree lands as
an ordinary change, in public, with its reason. Nothing is removed from the
tracker quietly.
